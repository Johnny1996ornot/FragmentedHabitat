How to play

Choose between a food-gathering journey and a race to colonize a new habitat. In both, survival depends on luck and strategy.

Start: each player has one animal and 5 food. Choose an animal, roll the movement die, then click neighbouring spaces. Move inward, outward or sideways. You may stop after one step; you do not have to use the whole roll. You may retrace your own steps, including returning to the square you just left. Each return step uses one movement point.

Classic — Add an animal (optional): before rolling, spend 2 food to add another animal at home. Its parent can be anywhere. You can have up to three living animals, but move only one per turn.

Choose your game

  • Classic — Habitat Fragmentation: animals start in the central habitat with 5 food per player. Cross the predator lanes and roads, collect fruit at the edge, then bring it back alive. The first player to bank the food target wins; if the patches empty, compare banked food plus carried fruit. You can choose a square, circular or octagonal board in Settings before starting.
  • Habitat Colonization: animals start at separate edge bases and make a one-way journey to the central habitat. Once an animal reaches it, it stays there and counts as a settler. It does not need to return. The first player to settle 6 animals wins. If the habitat fills first, the player with the most settlers wins; ties share the win. Its carrying capacity is 12 animals by default and can be changed in Settings. Settlers share the habitat safely and cannot be pushed or targeted. There are no food patches, hunters or golden harvests in this mode.

Colonization recruitment: add a new animal at your edge base for free before rolling. You can have up to three animals travelling at a time; settled animals do not count toward that limit. Your starting 5 food is used for revival. If you have no travelling animal, recruit one to take your turn.

Why carrying capacity matters: separate bases spread animals out at the start, but everyone is trying to reach the same habitat. That habitat can support only a limited number of settlers, so space becomes valuable. Arriving safely matters as much as choosing your route.

The landscape

Road: every time an animal stops on a light gray road space, it has to roll the day/night die.

  • Moon (night or odd): the animal is safe and stays where it is. In the game, night represents lighter traffic.
  • Sun (day or even): the animal gets hit by a car. Pay the revival cost to bring it back to its starting habitat or base.

Stopping on another road space later requires a new roll, even if the animal was safe last time. Passing through without stopping needs no roll.

Median strip: the dark gray island needs no traffic check. You can move along it in either direction or cross into a neighbouring lane. A traffic officer patrols this strip clockwise. If you stop on the officer, or it stops on your block, your animal returns to its starting habitat or base without paying food. Any carried fruit returns to its resource patch. After the predators move, roll once to move the officer.

Predator lanes: leaving the habitat or base exposes animals to predators. Players have numbered flags; predators do not. After everyone has taken a turn, click Move All Predators to run their turn. In Settings, choose one roll for all predators, one per lane, or one for each predator. You can also change the number per lane from 1 to 6; the default is 4. Predators always move clockwise. If one stops on your animal’s square, that animal is hit. You cannot voluntarily step onto a predator, but a rival can push you there. With individual rolls, predators may gather on the same space.

Resource patches (Classic): patches hold food at the edges. An ordinary patch gives 1 fruit to carry home. A golden horn patch gives 3 fruit, but you must face the hunter: moon/night means keep the fruit; sun/day means a hit. Each patch supplies only the first animal that collects from it. It stays empty unless its carried fruit is returned after a hit. Each animal collects from one patch per trip. In Colonization, the centre is a settlement habitat rather than a food patch.

Hits, revival and elimination

  • A predator, car, hunter or off-board hit sends the affected animal back to the habitat if you can revive it.
  • Any fruit it carries returns to the resource patch it came from. That patch can be collected again.
  • Pay 2 banked food to revive the animal at home.
  • If you cannot pay 2 food, only the hit animal leaves the board. Its carried fruit returns to the supply. You keep playing with your other surviving animals and keep your cards. You are eliminated only when you have no animals remaining. Then your cards return to the deck.
  • An eligible rescue card may be used before you pay. Play Dead keeps the animal and its fruit in place. Angel Conservationists revives it for free, but its fruit still returns to the patch.

Interspecific competition: land on another player’s animal to contest its space. The challenger chooses odd or even, and the other player gets the opposite. Roll once. The winner keeps the square and chooses an adjacent square to push the loser into. A push onto a road triggers a day/night check; a push onto a predator causes a hit. Other landing effects also apply. You cannot share a square outside the habitat or contest your own animals.

Risk rewards: winning a contest, surviving a car roll or surviving a hunter roll earns one card. Click the floating deck when prompted. Using Safe Streets or a revival card does not earn a survival reward.

Held cards: click the card icon beside a player’s name to view their cards, use an eligible card, or close the window. Cards drawn this round unlock next round.

  • 14 Reroll: replace your movement roll before taking a step.
  • 10 Extra Block: add one movement step.
  • 6 Move Enemy: choose an enemy, then move it one adjacent square in any direction. Occupied squares are unavailable. Landing consequences apply.
  • 4 Safe Streets: skip one traffic check. Future stops still need a check.
  • 2 Play Dead: survive a predator on the same square and keep your fruit. Only that predator rolls again.
  • 2 Angel Conservationists: revive at home free after a car or hunter hit; carried fruit returns to its original patch.

How Classic ends

  • Food target: the first player to reach the chosen banked-food target wins. The default is 10; change it in Settings.
  • Only one player remains: that player wins immediately, regardless of their food total.
  • No food remains in the patches: finish any pending hunter check and reward draw. If all patches are still empty, the player with the most banked food plus fruit carried by surviving animals wins. Equal totals share the win.
  • Returned fruit: food lost after a hit goes back to its original patch, so the board is not depleted while that food is available again.
  • Solo play: reach the target or the highest available depletion score; you lose only when you have no animals remaining.

Board controls: drag sideways to turn, vertically to tilt, and pinch or wheel to zoom. 3D View gives separate tilt, turn and Z rotation controls. Move Board lets you drag the whole board.
